# Social media output standard

Apply this to text, scripts, slides, visuals and videos intended for an audience.
Make public-facing outputs ready for the selected social channel by default.
Preserve the requested format and depth: a detailed tutorial still teaches the
full concept, and internal code, plans and manifests remain useful working files.
Do not add an unrequested campaign, publish the output, or replace a requested
long explanation with a teaser.

## Resolve the publishing destination

Use the platform and audience from the prompt or earlier answers. When missing
platform, format, length or audience changes the result, batch those questions
and wait at the intake breakpoint. If the requester delegates choices, record a
recommended destination and format with a reason. If several platforms are
requested, adapt each export or text version instead of assuming one file fits
every channel. Never silently crop a meaningful diagram or remove explanation.

Before final export, check current official platform requirements for aspect
ratio, duration, file size, codecs, captions, text limits and music eligibility
when relevant. Platform specifications and trend claims require current sources;
do not invent a limit, claim an old sound is trending, or promise algorithmic reach.

## Make the hook earn attention

Lead with a specific problem, relatable story, striking verified fact or useful
result. A video's opening image and first spoken line should communicate why the
viewer should continue; do not spend the opening on a slow logo intro. A post's
first line should stand alone and invite reading. Pay off the hook in the output
and make every beat add information, a cause-and-effect step or a meaningful
change. Prefer a clear focal idea over competing messages.

Aim for retention and shareability through useful explanations, emotional
recognition, visual clarity and a satisfying takeaway. Offer a natural reason to
save or share when appropriate. Keep questions and CTAs relevant to the content;
avoid engagement bait, false urgency, misleading thumbnails, fake success stories
and fabricated metrics. "Viral" is an objective, never a guaranteed outcome.

## Text that works in a feed

Write complete, ready-to-copy text for the chosen platform, with short paragraphs,
concrete language and a clear takeaway. Put essential information before optional
detail. Use hashtags and emoji only when relevant to the audience and channel;
do not add a fixed hashtag block, keyword stuffing or a sales CTA to every post.
Verify required links and claims. A programming post needs an accurate explanation,
not just catchy wording; keep code readable and label simplified examples.

## Video that works on a phone

Use the destination's layout, safe areas and export requirements. Keep the hook,
faces, objects, important code and Mermaid diagrams clear of platform controls
and caption overlays. Check the actual export on a phone-sized preview; avoid
tiny labels, dense paragraphs and details that require zooming to understand.
Use relevant visuals, labelled flows and smooth entrance → readable hold/action
→ exit. Time movement to meaning and narration, without overwhelming the viewer.

Make the main message understandable with sound off. Spoken social video gets
accurate, readable captions by default; derive cue timing from the final spoken
audio and review it. Silent video uses on-screen copy. If supplied footage must
be preserved or captions must be optional under an explicit request, follow that
request. Verify speech clarity and balanced music when audio is included.

Prepare a strong cover or thumbnail when it is part of the selected platform's
delivery. Keep it accurate to the content and legible at feed size. Do not treat
the source deck, storyboard or a static preview as proof the final video works.

## Final quality check

Verify the requested platform's format, readable text, correct crops, smooth
actual motion, captions, pacing, hook payoff, factual claims and CTA links.
Check the finished output rather than source declarations alone. Report only
checks actually performed. Future performance is unknown; use retention, saves,
shares, completion and relevant conversions to improve later versions when
real analytics are supplied. Never invent performance or present a draft as
already published.
