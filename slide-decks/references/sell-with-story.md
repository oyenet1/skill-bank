# Sell with a Story: working method

Use Paul Smith's *Sell with a Story* as the default storytelling method. This
reference paraphrases the selection and structure methods in chapters 12–17,
and applies the emotion, surprise, detail and truthfulness guidance in chapters
18–23. Write original stories grounded in the requester's material.

## Choose a story with a purpose

First resolve the intended change in the audience: what they should understand,
feel or be able to do afterwards. Look for a relevant real success, mistake or
discovery in the supplied material. Choose the incident whose protagonist, stakes
and obstacle the audience can recognize. If the requester wants an invented
teaching scenario, label it illustrative and keep the technical mechanism true.
Do not convert an invented example into a customer testimonial.

## Use the seven-part structure

| Part | What to establish | Application to post or video |
|---|---|---|
| Hook | Give the audience a reason to care about this incident. | Opening line and first visual establish relevant curiosity; the outcome remains earned. |
| Context | Establish the situation, protagonist and goal. | Use only the setting details needed to follow the events; show the relevant objects. |
| Challenge | Introduce the event or opportunity that changes the plan. | Make the initiating problem concrete, with a visible cause. |
| Conflict | Show the response, obstacles and consequences. | Connect attempts in causal order; show operations, inputs, outputs and labelled flows. |
| Resolution | Show how the situation ends. | Deliver an observable result that answers the hook and follows from the actions. |
| Lesson | Explain the useful meaning of the result. | State the principle clearly and distinguish an analogy from the actual mechanism. |
| Action | Connect the lesson to an appropriate next step. | Offer a relevant practice, decision or verified CTA; educational stories need not become sales pitches. |

These are narrative functions, not mandatory headings, seven paragraphs or seven
slides. Several can fit in one short beat; a complex conflict may need several
scenes. Preserve the causal chain and payoff while fitting the requested length.

## Make it engaging and credible

Use recognizable stakes and feelings connected to the character's situation.
Let a surprising discovery reveal the lesson when the incident supports it.
Choose a few concrete sensory details or short dialogue exchanges that help the
audience picture the event; remove background that delays understanding.
Attributed real dialogue needs a source; invented dialogue remains part of a
labelled illustrative scenario. Do not manufacture drama, quote a real person
without evidence, or promise results unsupported by the incident.

Tell the events as an experience someone can follow. A list of benefits is not a
story until there is a goal, disruption, response and consequence. A catchy hook
must connect to the actual resolution. End with meaning and an appropriate next
step rather than unrelated engagement bait.

## Apply it to programming and Slidev

Keep the same sequence for posts and Slidev videos. In the storyboard, record the
narrative function for each beat along with the visible object, action and timing.
Programming conflict beats show the real execution through Mermaid diagrams,
code or concrete state changes; resolution shows the actual corrected behavior.
Use smooth object entrances/exits and readable holds to help follow the story.

An illustrative teaching example could follow a developer whose login succeeds,
then fails after a session expires: show the request, rejection, refresh attempt
and retry, explain the actual protocol, then state the lesson and a practical
check. Keep the example consistent with the supplied system; do not imply every
authentication system uses refresh tokens.

Before delivery, check that the hook leads to the resolution, the events support
the lesson, and the requested action follows from it. Preserve this method when
shortening a story or adapting it into platform-specific text and narration.
