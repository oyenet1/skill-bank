# Marketing Playbook

The operating summary distilled from the sources in `references/library/marketing/`.
Use it to shape the **offer, angle, story and price** before writing a word of
copy. Search the library when you need depth on any single item — do not read a
whole book into context.

Every principle below is attributed. The library holds the full arguments.

---

## 1. Write the offer before the words

> *Alex Hormozi, $100M Offers*

- **You sell an offer, not a product.** The offer is the combination of what they
  get, what it costs, the risk they carry and the terms they pay on.
- **Commodity vs category of one.** A commodity is compared on price; a
  differentiated offer is compared to nothing. If the reader can say "these are
  the same, I'll take the cheaper one", the copy has already lost.
- **"The pain is the pitch."** Articulate the reader's pain accurately and they
  will buy the solution. Get the pain wrong and no amount of polish helps.
- A strong offer combines an attractive promotion, an unmatched value
  proposition, a premium price, a real guarantee, and payment terms that work.

## 2. The Value Equation

> *Alex Hormozi, $100M Offers*

```
Value = (Dream Outcome × Perceived Likelihood of Achievement)
        ────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
        (Perceived Time Delay × Perceived Effort & Sacrifice)
```

- **Raise the top, lower the bottom.** Most weak copy shouts the dream outcome
  and ignores the denominator.
- **The bottom is where the wins are.** Anyone can promise. Making it *immediate,
  seamless and effortless* is harder and far more valuable — Apple, Amazon and
  Netflix all won on the denominator.
- **Perception is reality.** An improvement the reader cannot perceive is not
  value. The London Underground's dotted "next train" map raised satisfaction
  more than faster trains would have, for a fraction of the cost.
- **Prefer psychological solutions to logical ones.** If a problem had an obvious
  logical fix, someone already shipped it.

**Writing checklist:** which single term does this piece move, and how does the
reader *perceive* the move?

## 3. Offer enhancers

> *Alex Hormozi, $100M Offers*

| Enhancer | Use |
|---|---|
| **Scarcity** | limited supply, honestly stated |
| **Urgency** | a real deadline, not a fake countdown |
| **Bonuses** | each one solves a named next problem |
| **Guarantee** | transfers risk from buyer to seller; makes saying yes safe |
| **Naming** | a named offer is memorable and harder to compare |

Every one of these is a claim. If the deadline is not real, do not print it.

## 4. Price signals value

> *Akin Alabi, How to Sell to Nigerians*

- He advertised a seminar at ₦15,000 and **nobody came**. He raised the price to
  ₦25,000 and **sold out** — with people at the door. Price changed the perceived
  value, not just the margin.
- **Price for maximum profitability, not minimum.** Under-pricing signals low
  value and attracts the wrong buyer.
- **The too-good-to-refuse offer.** In all three of his examples the offer, not
  the advertising, did the selling — including a bundle of products worth
  ₦100,000 sold for ₦4,500.

## 5. Leads and audience

> *Alex Hormozi, $100M Leads*

- **A lead is anyone you can contact. An engaged lead shows interest.** Only
  engaged leads are the point.
- **The Core Four** — every way to reach anyone:

  | | 1-to-1 | 1-to-many |
  |---|---|---|
  | **Warm** (gave permission) | Warm outreach | Posting content |
  | **Cold** (strangers) | Cold outreach | Paid ads |

  If leads are short, the answer is more skill or more volume in these four — not
  a new channel.
- **More, Better, New** — do more of what works, do it better, then add something
  new. In that order.
- **The lead magnet.** Valuable enough that you could charge for it; solves one
  *narrow* problem; and solving it reveals the next problem your core offer
  charges for. (Pretzels and drinks: the salty snack creates the thirst.)
- **Make it easy to consume.** Offering the same content in more formats
  multiplies reach for the same work — Hormozi's book splits near-evenly across
  ebook, print, audio and video.
- **Lead getters** — other people who bring leads: customers, employees,
  agencies, affiliates.

## 6. Story

> *Paul Smith, Sell with a Story*

- **Four foundations of a sales story:** a relatable hero, a relevant challenge,
  an honest struggle, and a worthy lesson. The **lesson** matters most.
- **Seven-stage structure:** hook (transition in) → context → challenge →
  conflict → resolution → lesson → action (transition out).
- **Choose the right story over telling it well.** A stutter is forgiven; an
  irrelevant story is not.
- **Emotion is necessary for a decision**, and **surprise** makes the lesson
  stick. Add both deliberately rather than hoping.
- **Never stretch the truth.** The credibility is the asset.

## 7. Build around demand

> *Akin Alabi, Small Business Big Money*

- **Do not build a product and then hunt for customers.** Find what people already
  want, then build the business around it. This is the most common
  first-business mistake.
- **Word of mouth is the cheapest engine** — engineer it rather than waiting.
- **A business is not fast cash.** If you need money now, get a job. Copy that
  implies overnight results is dishonest and attracts refunds.

## 8. Selling psychology

> *Brian Tracy, The Psychology of Selling*

- **The 80/20 rule:** the top 20% of sellers earn 80% of the commission.
- **Self-concept drives performance** — a seller who expects to succeed behaves
  differently.
- **Persistence pays:** most sales close after multiple contacts, not the first.
- **Enthusiasm transfers.** Flat copy reads as a flat offer.
- **Become a little bit better.** Small, compounding edges in skill produce large
  differences in outcome. Applies to copy: fix the hook, then the proof, then the
  CTA.

## 9. Trust and context

> *Akin Alabi, How to Sell to Nigerians*

- **Trust is the currency.** Build it before asking for anything; the advertising
  that failed was asking too early.
- **Local context matters** — payment language, distribution, price expectations
  and market reality are not universal.
- **Never transplant tactics across markets** without checking they fit. Name the
  context explicitly in the brief when the copy is market-specific.

## Before you write — the checklist

1. **What is the offer**, and why can it not be compared to anything else?
2. **Who is the reader**, what do they already believe, and what is the one action?
3. **Which Value Equation term** does this piece move — and is the move perceived?
4. **Which hook** fits their awareness stage (see `SKILL.md` §3)?
5. **What proof actually exists** — a real screen, sample, number or story?
6. **Does it need a story**, and which of the seven stages is missing?
7. **Is the price framing honest**, and does the offer itself do the selling?
8. **One CTA** to a real, existing destination.
9. **Never invent** a figure, review, deadline, testimonial or outcome.
